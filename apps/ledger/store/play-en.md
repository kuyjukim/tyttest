## App name (30)

Ledger - Envelope Budget

## Short description (80)

Budget before you spend, not after. One payment, no subscription, no network.

## Full description (4000)

There are plenty of apps that record what you already spent. Ledger decides before you spend.

Give each envelope an amount at the start of the month - groceries, transport, going out - and what is left stops being a guess. It is not an app that answers "how much do I have left this month?" so much as one that stops you having to ask.

■ Envelope budgeting, without the envelopes

The method is older than the phone: cash into labelled envelopes on payday, and when an envelope is empty, that category is done for the month. Zero-based budgeting is the same idea with every unit of income given a job. Both work and both are a nuisance to run on cash. Here the envelopes are in the app and you keep using the one card you already use - an envelope is just an allowance you set for yourself and can see the bottom of.

■ How much you can spend today

What is left, divided by the days that are left. Rounded down rather than up, so the number on screen is one you can actually spend every remaining day without running out.

■ Move money between envelopes

Short on groceries? Take it from somewhere else. That edits this month's plan, not your history - the record of what you actually bought stays exactly as it was.

■ Envelopes that carry over

For the things you save up for, what is left rolls into next month. So does an overspend. An envelope that forgives going over at midnight on the 31st is not a budget, it is a tally.

■ From payday to payday

Income arrives and gets divided straight away. Savings is an envelope you fill first, not whatever survives the month. Rent and the fixed bills sit in their own envelopes, and what is left over is what you can actually spend.

■ Money arithmetic that is actually correct

Decimal places follow the currency - none for won and yen, two for dollars, three for dinars - and every calculation is whole-number. Split an amount three ways and the parts still add back up to the total, to the last cent.

■ No subscription

Pay once. Using an expense tracker that takes money every month to manage the money that leaves every month never made much sense.

■ Offline, and not in the marketing sense

Not "we don't collect data" - there is no network code in the app. No account, no sign-in, no ads, no bank connection. The app sends your records nowhere, so it works on a plane and underground, and nothing here can stop working because a server did. If you have your phone’s own backup turned on, this app’s file is included in it like every other app’s - that is between you and Apple or Google, and the developer cannot see it.

■ Also

· Dark mode, following the system theme
· English and Korean
· Move freely between months; past and future months are both editable
· Spending by envelope, and a daily spending chart
· Archive an envelope to keep its history, or delete it outright
· Won, dollar, yen, euro, pound and dinar

■ What it does not do

There is no bank connection. That is a different kind of app, one that needs a server and a subscription to pay for it. This one asks you to enter things yourself, and in exchange what you entered is correct and yours for good.

Managing money comes down to small decisions made daily. All a budget app can do is make those decisions easier to take.
